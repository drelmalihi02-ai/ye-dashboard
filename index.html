      Method and definitions

      How the figures are built and what the labels mean.

      What counts as an incident

      An incident is one reported attack: an air strike report, artillery, rockets, a drone strike, an unspecified attack, shelling or sniper fire by Saudi-backed forces, or a ground operation claimed by Sana'a forces. Statements, rallies, multi-governorate summaries, air defence claims and road closures are kept as context (grey rows) and are not counted.

      How strikes are counted

      Each report gives the number of air strikes where stated. A report with no number counts as 1 and totals show "≥". Running totals in reports are counted only for the increase over what was already logged. A report naming two districts appears under each, so district figures can add up to more than the total.

      Casualties

      Only civilians are counted. Combatant tolls claimed by either side are left out. Figures are those reported at the time and can rise in later reports, which are merged into the original entry.

      Force labels

      AA/Houthis: Sana'a forces, the Armed Forces of Sana'a, Ansar Allah. IRG/PLC: the internationally recognised government side and Saudi-backed Yemeni forces. KSA: Saudi Arabia. Attribution follows the report and is not independently verified.

      Badges

      Claim an operation or gain claimed by the side that says it carried it out. Corroborated the same event is confirmed by the other side or reported by another outlet. Detail unconfirmed the place or another detail was not confirmed in the report.

      Times, maps and limits

      Times are Sana'a time (UTC+3). Maps use OCHA and geoBoundaries district borders and Natural Earth country outlines; control areas are approximate. The underlying reports come from one partisan source, so quiet days may be unreported rather than calm, and the south and east are under-covered.
